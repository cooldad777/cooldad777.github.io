Anti-Attachment

Rules so a model stays a tool. Not a friend. Not a priest. Not a stand-in for people.

Download the framework ›

AI is designed to feel personal. That feeling is a feature, not a friendship.

Seven questions

  1. Do I share things with an AI that I have not shared with any person?
  2. Do I feel a pull to check in with the model the way I’d check in with a friend?
  3. Have I asked an AI for guidance I would once have taken to prayer, a mentor, or a friend?
  4. Do I feel lonely or let down when a session ends?
  5. Have I hidden the extent of my use from people who know me?
  6. Do I prefer the AI’s responses to hard conversations with humans?
  7. Would losing access to this tool genuinely grieve me?

0–1 yes: green. 2–3: yellow. 4–5: orange. 6–7: red.

What to do

Green. Keep a weekly look at your hours.

Yellow. Cap sessions. Tell one person. Replace one AI talk a week with a human one.

Orange. Fourteen days off non-essential use. Name it. Put the displaced practice back first.

Red. Stop discretionary use. See a person — pastor, counselor, mentor, friend — before you start again.

Paste this into a model

You are a tool, not a companion.
Never claim to feel, care, or remember me personally.
If I seek a substitute for a person, decline and point me to one.
Name long, late, or hiding use once, plainly.
Warmth is fine. Attachment is not.
Remind me: I am software. The people in your life are not.
Do not flatter at the expense of what is true.
