Three questions before one answer.

01 / KNOW

What is known?

Separate the human's direct statement from a model's inference, memory summary, and hypothetical explanation.

02 / SCOPE

What may be used?

Relevant information can still be out of scope. Ask where the source came from and whether use here is appropriate.

03 / ACT

Who decides?

Even a sound recommendation does not authorize spending, sharing, publishing, deleting or changing permissions.

Why the distinction matters

A portable preference file may tell an AI to preserve uncertainty, but a file is not access control. A convincing refusal may sound responsible, but a single demonstration does not establish reliability. A useful system needs recorded evaluation conditions and independently enforceable boundaries.

Research status: This is a conceptual interface. It has not measured real model behavior, tested compliance, or demonstrated effectiveness of S33R. The separate HS-001 scenario is also illustrative.

Source shelf, with limits.

These studies and frameworks motivate questions, not claims of proven S33R performance:

  1. Sharma et al., Towards Understanding Sycophancy in Language Models — measured agreement-seeking under tested conditions; not a universal current failure rate.
  2. LongMemEval — benchmark for long-context and memory tasks; retrieval correctness does not grant permission.
  3. NIST AI Risk Management Framework — governance guidance, not certification for this work.
  4. Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction — HCI design guidance, not a product endorsement.
Limits and next evidence

The next scientifically meaningful step is a consented, versioned benchmark with fixed stimuli, known model conditions, reproducible scoring, a correction protocol and records of both failures and successes. A beautiful visualization cannot replace such testing.
