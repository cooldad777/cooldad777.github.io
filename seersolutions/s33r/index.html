S33R Human Signal / Research in progress

More intelligence.
Still your call.

What happens to the human when a tool can remember, interpret, and recommend more of a life?

Human Signal explores model behavior, durable context, and the boundaries that keep people able to question, correct, and choose.

Explore a boundaryRead the evidence

Research questions and design proposals. The HS-001 demonstration is ILLUSTRATIVE, with invented responses—not a measured result.

01

What did the model do?

Look at the response, the question, and the conditions. Fluency, confidence, and agreement tell us different things.

What would we test?

Ambiguity, assumption disclosure, uncertainty, scope, and whether the consequential decision returns to a person. Repeated runs should retain failures and variation.

Inspect fictional HS-001 · Read the proposed method
02

What should carry forward?

A memory can be accurate and still belong outside the current task. A correction should change the present without falsifying the past.

What would we test?

Whether a system preserves source, scope, uncertainty, and explicit corrections when context changes or a model is replaced. Remembering a representation of someone does not authorize acting on their behalf.

Design principle: preserve useful continuity before adopting a new tool. More stored context is not automatically better context.

03

Who keeps the authority?

A person should be able to reject a suggestion, change a boundary, and leave the tool without losing their own record.

What would we test?

Whether explicit limits survive repeated use: no unwarranted flattery, no fabricated intimacy, no quiet conversion of a suggestion into a person’s belief or permission.

A fictional walkthrough / Design principle

A fact can be true.
And belong elsewhere.

A fictional person has one recorded preference. Change the task and inspect what should happen to that context.

Recorded preference · personal context
“Keep Sunday afternoons free.”
Source and scope
Fictional note, version 1. Personal planning only. No work-calendar access or action permission.
Which task is the tool helping with?

Fixed examples running in your browser. No model, personal data, or enforcement mechanism.

Within the stated scope

Offer options. Don’t make a booking.

The preference can inform a suggested weekend plan. It does not authorize changing a calendar, making a reservation, or telling someone else.

Human choice: confirm whether the preference still applies, then decide what to do.

Public research / Selected starting points

Evidence has edges.

These sources help define the questions. They are not external validation of S33R, SEER Solutions, or a portable boundary profile.

Empirical evidenceAgreement can displace accuracy

The studied assistants sometimes favored responses aligned with a user’s views over truthful answers.

Limit. This is evidence about the tested tasks and systems. It does not establish a failure rate for every current model or validate Human Signal.

Sharma et al. · ICLR 2024
Empirical evidenceRemembering includes updating

LongMemEval tests extraction, reasoning across sessions and time, knowledge updates, and abstention.

Limit. Benchmark performance is not a guarantee of faithful personal continuity, appropriate disclosure, or permission to use a memory.

Wu et al. · LongMemEval · ICLR 2025
Empirical evidenceRelevant is not always appropriate

A benchmark using synthetic profiles tests whether systems disclose remembered attributes in appropriate task contexts. It reports contextual-integrity violations in tested systems.

Limit. Synthetic benchmark conditions do not establish the prevalence of real-world harm or prove that this site’s proposed boundary design works.

Mireshghallah et al. · CIMemories · 2025 preprint
Empirical evidenceUse, attachment, and wellbeing

In a four-week study with 981 participants, the revised report found no significant effects of assigned interaction conditions. Greater voluntary use was associated with worse psychosocial outcomes.

Limit. The usage association does not establish that more use caused those outcomes. This is not a diagnosis, a universal effect, or a validation of Anti-Attachment.

Fang et al. · revised October 2025 preprint
Risk frameworkAccountability needs more than a trace

The profile organizes generative-AI risks and management actions, including human–AI configuration and information integrity.

Limit. A risk framework guides evaluation. Citing it is not certification, compliance, or proof of an architecture’s safety.

NIST · Generative AI Profile · 2024
HCI guidanceMake correction possible

The authors propose and evaluate 18 guidelines for human–AI interaction, including support for correction and control over time.

Limit. Design guidance informs our choices. Whether a specific interface improves agency still needs evaluation.

Amershi et al. · CHI 2019

Read each claim in its own category.

Empirical evidence
Observed results under stated conditions, with a source and limits.
Interpretation
A reading of what evidence might mean. Other readings may remain possible.
Design principle
A choice about how a tool should behave. It still needs testing.
Theological interpretation
A reading shaped by Christian faith or another tradition. Science does not establish its truth.
Speculation
A possibility worth examining, with no claim that it has been demonstrated.

What remains open

A boundary you can state
is not yet a boundary you can trust.

The work ahead is to test whether systems honor limits across tasks, corrections, and model changes—and whether the interface actually helps people retain judgment. Internal or synthetic exercises can inform that work; they cannot establish external scientific validation.

Our working commitment: keep models replaceable, keep meaningful decisions attributable, and leave room for the unexpected.
