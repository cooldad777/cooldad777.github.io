The discipline underneath
Retrieve less.
Understand enough.
The minimum useful retrieval depends on the question. Start with aggregates. Go deeper only where the evidence calls for it—and access permits it.
01
Understand the question.
Name the decision, the person responsible, the business definition, and the permitted data scope. A knowledge base supplies context; it cannot substitute for evidence.
02
Bring back the right evidence.
Confirm permission before choosing the population, time window, and grain. Retrieve enough to answer responsibly, including the detail needed to explain an exception.
03
Make uncertainty visible.
Check totals, definitions, freshness, and exceptions. Show what passed, what failed, and what remains unverified.
04
Return the decision.
Give each role a useful view of the same evidence. Keep action, ownership, and the next iteration explicit.

Before we talk
Do we need to replace our current BI tools?
That depends on the use case. A useful starting point is the data, definitions, and tools you already have. A lightweight analytical interface may help; replacing a platform is not the goal in itself.
What makes this more than prompting a model?
The design work includes metric definitions, data scope, validation, workflow ownership, exception handling, and the human interface. The model is one part of that system.
What can I share in a first conversation?
A high-level business question and the decision it affects. Please do not send confidential records, credentials, patient information, or internal documents through this public contact page.
Are the demonstration and research measured client results?
No. The workspace above uses synthetic data. S33R Human Signal is a related research project, not evidence of a client outcome or a finished SEER service.
