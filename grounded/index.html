Christian formation for men in an age of engineered attention

GROUNDED

You were not made to fight the feed alone.

A small weekly practice: phones outside, men in a room, Scripture open, silence long enough to notice what has our hearts, and one concrete commitment at a time.

These are scale markers, not a sum, and not a claim that every dollar is spent to keep people scrolling. Alphabet and Meta R&D includes broad product, infrastructure, and AI work. The point is simpler: individual willpower operates inside systems financed and optimized at extraordinary scale.

GROUNDED does not answer that with panic. It answers with formation, practice, and other people.

The north star

The goal is not less technology. The goal is a life more deeply formed by Jesus.

If our love is rightly ordered, technology becomes a discipleship question instead of a screen-time contest.

“I would never do this consistently on my own.” The lesson that carried over from F3

Why together

The insight came from doing hard things with other men.

F3 taught me something I could feel in my body. I would show up for difficult workouts with other men that I would not reliably choose alone. There was regularity, shared effort, rotating leadership, conversation, and a Circle of Trust at the end.

Free Peer-led Rotating leadership Circle of Trust

The lesson was not “try harder.” It was that community changes what a man can faithfully sustain. GROUNDED applies that lesson to attention and spiritual formation.

Most of us are used to acting like lone wolves. This is deliberately the opposite.

Why practice

Trying harder is not the same as becoming different.

“Disciplines are ... activities in our power that enable us, by grace, to do what we cannot do by direct effort.”
Dallas Willard · How Does the Disciple Live?

Willard’s insight fits GROUNDED almost exactly: formation is not merely deciding harder. We arrange our bodies, habits, time, relationships, and attention so that grace can meet us where direct willpower fails.

That is why this is not a lecture about technology, and not a tech-detox contest. It is an embodied Christian practice: solitude, silence, Scripture, prayer, fellowship, accountability, and small experiments in obedience.

The weekly rhythm

What happens in the room.

No guru. No performance. One man can guide the rhythm each week, then someone else can guide it next time.

  1. 01

    Arrive

    Personal smartphones stay outside the room. We come in as people, not as notification endpoints.

  2. 02

    Notice

    We talk honestly about what technology has been doing to our attention, desire, prayer, work, relationships, and ability to be present. No shame score. No screen-time contest.

  3. 03

    Read

    We practice a simple Lectio Divina rhythm with a short passage of Scripture: read, reflect, respond, rest. Slowly. With silence between words.

  4. 04

    Share

    What did you notice? What stirred? What felt difficult? We listen before we fix. The point is not to manufacture insight or perform spirituality.

  5. 05

    Commit

    One small practice for the week. Something concrete enough that another man can ask about it next time.

Outside the room

Personal smartphones

Inside the room

People, Scripture, silence

Plus one simple emergency line.

Emergency line: a basic prepaid call-and-text phone can stay in the room. Families get the number beforehand. It exists for urgent contact, not browsing.

Why silence matters

Instant answers can become a substitute for discernment.

An LLM is not a spirit. The concern is more ordinary: a fluent, personalized system can become the first place we turn every time uncertainty opens.

The instant-answer reflex

  • Always available
  • Fast and articulate
  • Personalized to your language
  • Ready to fill silence immediately
  • Often easier than waiting, asking, or wrestling

The GROUNDED practice

  • Scripture
  • Silence
  • Prayer
  • Waiting
  • Embodied Christian community

The temptation is not that AI literally becomes the Holy Spirit. It is that instant, personalized language can begin doing work we should sometimes reserve for Scripture, prayer, the Spirit, wise people, and patient discernment.

Lectio Divina is not magic, and the group is not trying to manufacture a voice from God. We read Scripture slowly, pray, wait, listen, and make space to respond together.

The better question

Minutes are not the whole measure.

A long day at a computer for work is not automatically worse than ten compulsive minutes of scrolling. GROUNDED asks what the technology is forming in us.

Was this use chosen, or was it automatic?
Did it make me more available to God and the people in front of me?
Was I creating, learning, connecting, working, escaping, or numbing?
Did I use the tool, or did the tool set the agenda?
What happens in me when I have to wait without filling the silence?
What would a relationship with technology that glorifies God actually look like?

Start small

One small group. Once a week. Four weeks.

The first goal is not to scale GROUNDED. It is to learn whether this practice helps men become more present to Jesus and to one another.

One shared weekly commitment. Ask about it next week. Adjust the practice. Keep what bears good fruit.
